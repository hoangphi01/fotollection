### How the suggestions are made, and scored

First the moments are found. Photos are ordered by the time they were taken, to the fraction of a second, and a new moment starts when the gap is longer than 2 seconds or four times the usual gap, when the camera or the lens changes, or when the place moves by more than 30 m. Each photo also gets a small visual signature, its layout in light and dark and its mix of colours, and a shot joins the moment only if it still looks like the shots before it, allowing for a step to the side or a zoom that the focal length explains.

Then, within a moment, every photo is measured on a reduced copy: the sharpness of the subject, camera shake, exposure (clipped highlights, crushed shadows, brightness), noise, and, where there are faces, whether the eyes are open and the faces turned to the camera. Each measure is compared only with the other shots of the same moment, and weighted (subject sharpness 35 %, open eyes 20 %, shake 15 %, exposure 15 %, clipped highlights 10 %, the rest less), with a smallest step so that a difference too small to see in a print never decides. The suggestion comes with its reason, nothing is deleted, and everything runs on your computer in 17 ms a photo, 28 ms with faces.

To keep it honest, 95 real photos were sorted by hand into 22 moments, with the photo to keep for the 6 moments that had a clear best. Each improvement was scored against that:

| Step | Moments found (of 22) | Wrong merges | Acceptable picks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Image fingerprint only | 3 | 0 | 2 of 2 |
| Plus layout and colour | 13 | 0 | 3 of 4 |
| Plus zoom from the focal length | 15 | 0 | 5 of 6 |
| Plus fair sharpness and hidden faces | 15 | 0 | 6 of 6 |

Two different moments are never grouped together; the two extra groups are pairs marked as unclear by hand. The moments still missed are ones where the pose or the framing changes a lot, such as a dish photographed again from the other side.
